# Pilot Readiness

## Current faculty workflows

Faculty can begin with a syllabus, an assignment, or an empty course. The shared workspace then supports Outcomes → Assessment Evidence → Learning Experiences/Practice → Alignment → Course Design Story. Returning faculty use the dashboard and its single contextual next step to resume work.

## What the Course Design Partner can do

- Parse clearly structured syllabus sections locally for faculty review.
- Preserve source material while faculty confirm, revise, and connect outcomes, assessments, assignments, and learning experiences.
- Offer curated, deterministic design patterns and practice recommendations.
- Show Outcome → Practice → Evidence pathways and a compact matrix.
- Retain faculty-approved decisions in a local Course Design Story.
- Provide transparent-assignment/TiLT scaffolding and optional external-AI prompt handoffs.

## Intentional limits

This prototype does not provide authentication, institutional storage, program-level design, course-quality scores, automatic course redesign, paid AI calls, or discipline-specific interpretation of unstructured documents. Recommendations are starting points for faculty judgment.

## Privacy behavior

Course records, parsed text, decisions, and pilot feedback are stored in browser local storage. Files are extracted for the prototype record; originals are not retained. AI is disabled, and no faculty material is sent to OpenAI. Optional prompt handoffs are copied by the faculty member to a tool of their choice.

## Known prototype limitations

Browser storage is device- and browser-specific, has no collaboration or recovery service, and can be cleared by the user. The single-page workspace has no shareable deep links to an individual outcome or pathway. Production deployment should continue to be checked after each release because persistence intentionally depends on browser storage.

## Suggested pilot tasks

1. Upload a clearly structured syllabus and accept or edit one proposed outcome.
2. Revise one outcome, connect assessment evidence, and design one practice experience.
3. Inspect the resulting pathway and Course Design Story.
4. Upload an existing assignment and use Standard Review and Transparent Design.
5. Return after refresh and resume the suggested next step.

## Suggested participant mix

Recruit 6–10 faculty across introductory/advanced courses, delivery modalities, disciplines with different assignment types, and both faculty revising an existing course and those planning a new one. Include participants with varied familiarity with backward design and active learning.

## Feedback questions

- Did the workspace help you make a meaningful course-design decision?
- Did the recommendations feel relevant enough to explore?
- Was it clear which information came from your source material and which you approved?
- Where did you hesitate or feel unsure what to do next?
- Would you use this when revising a course? What is missing?

## Observation issues to watch

Watch for repeated entry of known context, confusion between learning experiences and individual activities, over-reliance on parser proposals, unnoticed local-storage limitations, difficult mobile matrix scanning, and navigation away from a selected outcome or assessment without context.

## Proceed-to-next-phase criteria

Proceed when most participants can complete a meaningful pathway without facilitator help, understand the Design Story as their own work, successfully return after refresh, and identify a clear next action. Prioritize fixing repeated confusion, lost context, accessibility barriers, or source-versus-current-design ambiguity before adding another major capability.
